Roof Leak Water Damage in Decatur: What to Know

Where you see the stain and where the rain gets in are usually two different places. Rain slips under a lifted shingle, a cracked flashing joint or a tired skylight seal, then runs along the decking and rafters before it drops onto the insulation. By the time a brown ring shows on the ceiling, the attic above it has often been wet for a while. Our team maps everything that got wet, then dries it.

We are a water damage crew, not a roofing company. Inside the home, our team takes out saturated insulation, dries the wood and drywall, treats any mold and repairs the ceiling. Should the leak still be active, our crew can cover that section with a tarp for now, or time our work with the roofing contractor you pick so nothing gets sealed over an open leak.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Shut Off Wet CircuitsIf water is dripping through a light fixture, a ceiling fan or near an outlet, switch off that circuit at the breaker and leave the fixture alone.
  2. Catch the DripsPut a pan or bucket beneath each drip, and shift rugs, electronics and furniture clear of the wet area. Protect the floor with plastic sheeting or old towels.
  3. Drain a Bulging CeilingA ceiling that sags with trapped water can be drained: stay off to the side and make a small opening at its lowest spot, with a bucket underneath.
  4. Stay Off the RoofA wet roof and an unlit attic are both places to stay out of. From indoors, mark where the water enters, and let professionals handle everything else.
  5. Document What You SeeBefore anything is moved or discarded, get video and photos of the attic, the ceiling and damaged items, and then phone us so drying can begin.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

When a leak happens suddenly and by accident, for example a branch going through the roof or wind stripping shingles, many homeowners policies pay for the water damage inside. Insurers generally turn down claims tied to wear, age or lack of upkeep, and they may handle the roof repair on different terms than the inside damage. Photos and moisture readings from us go to the adjuster, though only your policy decides coverage, and your agent can explain it.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • Nails that have backed out of the decking and left small holes through the shingles.
  • A skylight whose curb or seal has dried and cracked, so rain gets past it.
  • A fallen limb or large hail that punctures or bruises the roof covering.
  • Roof valleys where worn metal or piled debris lets water get under the covering.
  • Gaps at chimneys, vents and sidewalls where the flashing has separated or rusted through.

What the Water Damages

Ceiling Fixtures and Wiring

Recessed cans, ceiling fans and electrical boxes are low points where roof water collects and drips through. Keep that circuit off until someone has inspected it, since damp wiring may corrode or short out.

Insulation in the Attic

Wet insulation mats down, stops insulating and holds water against the ceiling drywall for days. In some cases fiberglass batts dry out and stay. Removal is the usual answer for wet cellulose.

Ceiling Drywall

Staining, sagging and loss of strength follow when drywall is not dried. Seams split, the tape lets go, and a heavy enough section can drop with little warning.

Wall Cavities

From the attic, a leak can drop into a stud bay and soak the wall insulation and drywall from behind. You might notice nothing more than a peeling patch or a puffy baseboard.
